Embryological Foundations

Organogenesis & The 3 Germ Layers

During gastrulation, the blastula differentiates into three embryonic germ layers. In Tibb, each layer maps directly to primary organ systems and governing temperamental qualities.

External Layer Tibb: Brain Center

Ectoderm

Dominant Quality: Moistness (Rutoobat)

Derived Structures

  • Nervous System & Brain Neurons
  • Epidermis & Skin Surface
  • Pigment Cells & Sensory Precursors
  • Mammary Gland Antecedents
Tibb Principle:

Forms nervous structures and sensory apparatus essential for psychological (Nafsani) functions.

Middle Layer Tibb: Heart Center

Mesoderm

Dominant Quality: Dryness (Yaboosat)

Derived Structures

  • Cardiac, Skeletal & Smooth Muscles
  • Dermis Layer & Bone Marrow
  • Blood Cells & Cardiovascular Tubules
  • Renal Tubules & Urogenital Apparatus
Tibb Principle:

Powers vital dynamics (Rooh-e-Hayawani) and mechanical contractile structures.

Internal Layer Tibb: Liver Center

Endoderm

Dominant Quality: Heat (Hararat)

Derived Structures

  • Gastrointestinal Tract Lining
  • Liver, Gallbladder & Pancreatic Cells
  • Alveolar Lungs & Respiratory Lining
  • Thyroid Cells & Upper Urogenital Epithelium
Tibb Principle:

Governs metabolic assimilation (Tabi'at) and digestive humour generation.

Biological Levels of Structural Hierarchy

Level 1

Muscle Cell / Unit

Individual functional cell derived from germ stem lineage.

Level 2 (Az'a-e-Basit)

Muscle Tissue

Cells with mutual embryonic origin grouped into tissues.

Level 3 (Az'a-e-Aliyah)

Organ (Bladder / Heart)

Two or more tissues integrated for specific primary functions.

Level 4

Organ System

Interrelated organs (e.g., Kidney, Ureter, Bladder, Urethra).

Histology & Temperamental Qualities

The Four Primary Tissues

Every organ in the human body is formed by a unique combination of four tissues. Each tissue carries distinct physiological characteristics and temperamental balances.

Cold & Dry

Connective Tissue

Binds cells and organs together. Provides structural protection, support, and integration. Includes blood, loose connective, adipose, cartilage, dense tissue, and bone.

Fat & Padding Bone Tendons & Cartilage
Dry & Hot

Muscular Tissue

Excitable in nature, responding to stimuli through contraction and relaxation for body movement. Classified into skeletal, smooth, and cardiac muscular tissue.

Cardiac Muscle Skeletal Muscle Smooth Muscle
Hot & Dry to Hot & Moist

Epithelial Tissue

Sheets of cells covering exterior body surfaces, lining internal cavities, and forming glands. Includes squamous, cuboidal, columnar, ciliated, transitional, and stratified keratinised.

Squamous (Heart) Cuboidal (Kidneys) Columnar (Intestines)
Moist & Hot to Cold & Moist

Nervous Tissue

Excitable tissue facilitating electrochemical signal propagation. Communicates sensory and motor directives between brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves.

Brain Neurons Spinal Cord Peripheral Nerves

Criteria Determining Organ Temperament

Tissue Ratio The qualitative balance or composition of different tissue types within the organ.
Blood Supply The degree of vascularization directly influences intrinsic heat and moistness levels.
Organ Dynamics Active physiological movement or contractility amplifies metabolic heat and dryness.

Taxonomy of Aa'za

Classification of Organs

Organs in Unani Tibb medicine are systematically categorized across three foundational dimensions: Structure, Innate Powers, and Biological Function.

Homogeneous Organs

Simple Organs (Aa'za-e-basit / Mufridah)

Organs in which every visible part carries the same name, structure, and definition as the whole organ.

Examples:
Cartilage Ligament Tendon Membrane Fat Muscle Nerve Bone
Heterogeneous Instruments

Compound Organs (Aa'za-e-aliyah / Murakbah)

Complex organs whose constituent parts differ in structure, nature, and name from the whole organ. Referred to as instruments of expression.

Examples:
Hand & Feet Stomach Brain Liver Kidneys Intestines

Organs possess innate faculties (Quwwat) for food absorption, retention, assimilation, and integration. Classification according to receiving and donating powers (Table 17):

Az'u Qabil Muat

Receptors & Effectors

Receive power and also donate power to other organs.

e.g., Brain & Liver (Receive life faculty & heat from Heart, donate sensations & nutrients).

Az'u Muat'i Ghair Qabil

Effectors Only

Donates its innate power to other organs without receiving from others.

e.g., Heart (Sole supreme donor of vital force & innate heat).

Az'u Qabil Ghair Muat'i

Receptors Only

Receives power from donor organs but has no faculty to bestow to others.

e.g., Muscles (Receive sensations from Brain and vital force from Heart).

Az'u Ghair Qabil

Passive / Neither

Purely structural elements without active power transmission.

e.g., Primary structural matrix like cartilage or simple padding fat.

Principal Organs (Aa'za Ra'eesa)

Absolutely essential for the survival of the individual and preservation of the human race. Centers of primary biological dynamics.

For Individual Preservation:

  • Heart: Center of vital force (Rooh-e-Hayawani)
  • Liver: Center of nutrition & blood formation (Nutritive Faculty)
  • Brain: Center of sensation, cognition & movement (Psychic Faculty)

For Race Preservation:

Testicles / Ovaries: Center of reproduction and continuation of lineage.

Auxiliary Organs (Aa'za Khadima)

Subordinate organs supporting principal centers, split into preparatory support and functional instruments.

a) Auxiliary to Vital Organs (Khidmat-e-muhaeya):

Prepares raw materials for principal organs (e.g., Lungs serve Heart; Stomach serves Liver).

b) Instruments of Functions (Khidmat-e-moodeya):

Executes directives of principal organs (e.g., Arteries serve Heart; Nerves serve Brain).

The Core Triad

Heart, Liver & Brain Theory of Organs

The principal players in the human body are completely integrated in terms of energy transfer and information supply. An imbalance in their connection triggers physical or psychical diseases.

The Vital Group

The Heart (Qalb)

Controls Cardiovascular & Respiratory Systems • Size of one's fist

Egyptian Theory: Seat of intellect, sentiment, and human consciousness.
Aristotle & Hippocrates: Supreme organ emerging first at body center. Root of all power and innate heat.
William Harvey: King of the body driving blood circulation through continuous pump action.
The Metabolic Group

The Liver (Jigar)

Governs Digestive, Excretory & Reproductive Systems

Ancient Repository: Repository of life due to immense mass, heat, and role in humor (Akhlat) synthesis.
Galen's View: First organ formed in fetus; seat of nutritive faculties producing four primary humors.
Bartholin Discovery: Shifted view by proving liver produces bile and operates as metabolic powerhouse.
The Psychic Group

The Brain (Dimaag)

Reigns Central & Peripheral Nervous Systems

Early Egyptian View: Regarded as cranial stuffing (word first recorded in Edwin Smith surgical papyrus).
Galen's Cognition Theory: Center where five senses meet and integrate into common sense (Hiss-e-Mushtarak).
Ventricle Integration: Anterior ventricle (Imagination) → Middle (Reasoning) → Posterior (Memory).
